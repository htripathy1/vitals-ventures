How a quarter works

  1. 1
    Treat patients

    Every quarter, patients arrive with different insurance. Insured care is profitable, uninsured care loses money but has the most community impact. Same treatment, very different finances.

  2. 2
    Manage the budget

    Pay staff, handle events and make investments. Every dollar you spend is a dollar you can't spend elsewhere, which is opportunity cost.

  3. 3
    Handle surprises

    Flu surges, equipment failures, Medicaid rate cuts and storms. Healthcare finance is unpredictable, so your cash reserve is your protection.

  4. 4
    Balance the score

    The composite score is 40% financials, 40% community health and 20% reputation. Maximizing only one leads to failure.

Finance concepts built into every decision

Payer mix
The ratio of insured, Medicaid and uninsured patients, which controls revenue.
Cash runway
How many quarters until you run out of cash at your current burn rate.
Operating margin
Revenue minus total costs per quarter. Negative means cash is being burned too quickly.
Cross-subsidy
Profits from insured patients help fund care for uninsured ones.
Opportunity cost
Choosing one thing means giving up another. Every hire is equipment you can't buy.
Revenue diversification
Grants, insurance and Medicaid are three income streams. Lose one and the others have to compensate.
